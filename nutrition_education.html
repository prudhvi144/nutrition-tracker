📚 Key Definitions

Recommended Dietary Allowance RDA

The average daily dietary nutrient intake level sufficient to meet the nutrient requirements of nearly all (97.5%) healthy individuals in a particular life stage and gender group. It is intended for healthy individuals to satisfy nutritional needs with a very small risk of inadequacy and is set at the 97.5th percentile of requirements.

🌱 Nutrition Basics

What Your Body Needs

Your body requires over 40 different nutrients to function optimally. These include:

  • Macronutrients: Carbohydrates, proteins, and fats needed in large amounts for energy and growth
  • Micronutrients: Vitamins and minerals needed in smaller amounts for various body functions
  • Water: Essential for all body processes and makes up about 60% of body weight

ICMR-NIN 2020 Guidelines

The Indian Council of Medical Research - National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN) released updated dietary guidelines in 2020, specifically tailored for the Indian population. These guidelines consider:

  • Indian body composition and metabolic differences
  • Local food availability and dietary patterns
  • Climate and lifestyle factors specific to India
  • Updated scientific research and global standards

🥖 Macronutrients

Carbohydrates - Your Body's Main Fuel

Recommended: 45-65% of total daily calories (372.6g for men, 292.9g for women)

Carbohydrates are your body's preferred energy source. Focus on complex carbohydrates from whole grains, millets, and vegetables rather than simple sugars.

Proteins - Building Blocks of Life

Recommended: 54g for men, 45.7g for women (0.83g per kg body weight)

Essential for tissue repair, enzyme production, and immune function. Include a variety of protein sources like dal, paneer, eggs, and lean meats.

Fats - Essential for Health

Recommended: 25g visible fat for sedentary individuals

Fats are crucial for hormone production, vitamin absorption, and cell membrane health. Include healthy fats from nuts, seeds, and oils.

💊 Vitamins

Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

These vitamins are stored in body fat and don't need to be consumed daily. However, deficiencies can develop over time.

Water-Soluble Vitamins (B-complex, C)

These vitamins are not stored in the body and need regular replenishment through diet. Excess amounts are excreted in urine.

Key Vitamin Considerations for Indians

  • Vitamin D: Often deficient due to limited sun exposure and dietary sources
  • Vitamin B12: Important for vegetarians who may have limited animal product intake
  • Folate: Essential for women of childbearing age

⚗️ Minerals

Major Minerals

Needed in larger amounts (>100mg/day): Calcium, Phosphorus, Magnesium, Sodium, Potassium, Chloride

Trace Minerals

Needed in smaller amounts (<100mg/day): Iron, Zinc, Copper, Manganese, Iodine, Selenium

Common Deficiencies in India

  • Iron: Especially in women due to menstruation
  • Calcium: Important for bone health, especially with limited dairy intake
  • Zinc: Essential for immune function and wound healing
  • Iodine: Prevented through iodized salt usage

📋 Meal Planning Guide

Daily Meal Structure

  • Breakfast (20-25% of daily calories): Include protein, complex carbs, and healthy fats
  • Lunch (30-35% of daily calories): Balanced meal with all food groups
  • Dinner (25-30% of daily calories): Lighter than lunch but nutritionally complete
  • Snacks (10-15% of daily calories): Nutrient-dense options like fruits, nuts, yogurt

Indian Food Groups to Include Daily

  • Cereals & Millets: Rice, wheat, ragi, bajra, jowar
  • Pulses & Legumes: Dal, rajma, chana, sprouts
  • Vegetables: Green leafy, colorful varieties, seasonal produce
  • Fruits: Seasonal, local varieties for vitamins and fiber
  • Dairy: Milk, curd, paneer for protein and calcium
  • Fats & Oils: Ghee, mustard oil, coconut oil in moderation
